420-order model, data dictionary, star schema, 8 governed KPIs and a live interactive dashboard.

Output delivery-risk, supplier and overdue-cash exposure in one weekly view — every figure traceable to source.

420 orders across projects, suppliers, delivery milestones, invoices and payments — with no single view of where delivery or cash was at risk.

360 invoices turned into aging, a DSO proxy, promise coverage and a cash-priority queue.

Output a ranked list of which invoices to chase first, by expected cash and customer risk.

360 invoices across 48 customers, with collections chasing by invoice age rather than by recoverable cash.
A formula-driven Excel command center — aging, overdue exposure, a DSO proxy, promise coverage and a cash-priority queue — plus a customer risk summary, a governed metric dictionary and reusable SQL.
88.7% of the $949k receivables book was already overdue, and $322k of that sat in the 90+ day bucket. Against a 55.7-day DSO proxy, open payment promises covered only 30.5% of overdue exposure — most of the cash at risk had no committed date behind it. Disputes, broken promises and aging risk are scored separately so the queue chases expected cash, not invoice age.

300 purchase orders scored for spend, OTIF, fill rate, price variance, defects and supplier risk.

Output every supplier sorted into a risk tier, with the drivers behind each score.

300 purchase orders across 20 suppliers, with supplier reviews argued from anecdotes rather than evidence.
A scorecard rating spend, OTIF, fill rate, delay, price variance and defects. Price, delivery and quality stay separate before combining into a transparent risk score. Plus a category-spend view and reusable SQL.
OTIF ran at 37.7% while fill rate held at 98.9% and defects at 0.64% — suppliers were shipping the right goods, an average of 4.0 days late. Four suppliers carrying $1.94m of the $9.61m spend scored 0% OTIF. Naming that as a lead-time problem, not a quality one, points corrective action at schedules instead of blanket pressure.
